What Should a Professional Company Profile Include?

A company profile should give prospective clients a clear, confident picture of your business. Here are the sections every professional profile needs.

By Lehlohonolo Thomas Maishoane · Published 22 October 2026 · Updated 23 October 2026

A company profile is often the document a prospective client, procurement officer or partner reads before deciding whether to engage with your business. It should give them a clear, confident picture of who you are, what you do and why you can be trusted with the work.

Good company profile design is not about filling pages. It is about presenting accurate information in a structure that busy readers can scan quickly.

Who reads a company profile?

Before deciding what to include, consider who will read the document. Corporate buyers, procurement teams, potential partners and larger clients usually want to confirm a few things quickly: that your business is legitimate, that you offer what they need, that you have the capacity to deliver and that they can reach you easily. A business profile designed around those questions is far more useful than one that lists everything the business has ever done.

The essential sections of a company profile

Most professional company profiles include the following sections. Adapt the order and depth to your industry.

1. Cover and introduction

A clean branded cover with your logo, business name and a short description of what you do. The introduction or company overview explains, in a few sentences, who you are, when the business was established and who you serve.

2. Vision, mission and values

Keep these short and specific. One clear sentence is better than a paragraph of general statements.

3. Services or products

Describe each service in terms of what the client receives. Group related services together and use headings so the reader can find what is relevant to them.

4. Capabilities and capacity

Explain the type and scale of work you are equipped to handle, your equipment or resources where relevant, and your areas of operation.

5. Experience and relevant projects

Include genuine project information that you have permission to share. Never invent clients or results; inaccurate claims damage credibility as soon as they are checked.

6. Leadership and team

Introduce the key people and their roles. Corporate readers want to know who is responsible for delivery.

7. Compliance and registration details

Depending on your industry, this may include company registration, tax and B-BBEE information, professional memberships or certifications. Include only what applies to your business and keep it up to date.

8. Contact details

Make it easy to take the next step: phone number, email address, physical or service area, and website.

What makes company profile design effective

Corporate company profiles are judged on presentation as well as content. The most effective profiles share a few qualities:

  • A consistent brand identity throughout, matching your logo, colours and stationery
  • Clear hierarchy, so headings, key facts and contact details stand out
  • Concise writing that focuses on client value rather than long paragraphs
  • High-quality, relevant imagery rather than unrelated stock photographs
  • A format that works both printed and as a PDF sent by email

Company profile design in South Africa: common mistakes

Some of the most common issues we see are outdated contact details, inconsistent branding, pages of dense text, missing compliance information and designs that look different from the rest of the business's materials. Each of these makes the reader work harder, which is the opposite of what a company profile should do.
