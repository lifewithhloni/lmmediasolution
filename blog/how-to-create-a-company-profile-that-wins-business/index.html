How to Create a Company Profile That Wins Business Opportunities

When you pursue tenders and corporate clients, your company profile makes the first impression. Here is how to make it work harder for your business.

By Lehlohonolo Thomas Maishoane · Published 22 October 2026 · Updated 23 October 2026

When a business pursues a tender, a supplier opportunity or a corporate client, its documents often make the first impression. Before anyone meets you, they may read your company profile, capability statement or proposal. Professional company profile design will not replace capability, but it ensures your capability is understood.

Start with the opportunity, not the template

Different opportunities call for different documents. Before you begin, ask:

  • Who will read this document, and what decision are they making?
  • What information have they asked for, and in what format?
  • What do they need to see to feel confident shortlisting you?

A general company profile introduces the business. A capability statement, tender submission or proposal must answer a specific need.

Gather accurate source information

Strong documents are built on accurate information. Collect the following before design begins:

  • A concise business description and the services relevant to the opportunity
  • Company registration, compliance and certification details that apply to your business
  • Leadership and key personnel, with their roles
  • Relevant projects you have permission to reference
  • Equipment, resources or capacity information where relevant
  • Current contact details

Never exaggerate experience or invent clients. Procurement teams verify information, and inaccuracies can disqualify a submission.

Structure the profile for quick reading

Decision makers review many documents. Make yours easy to scan:

  • Use clear headings that match the reader's questions
  • Lead each section with the most important point
  • Use short paragraphs, lists and simple tables for key facts
  • Keep contact details easy to find

Add a corporate capability statement

A corporate capability statement is a concise, often one or two page summary of what your business can deliver. It typically covers core competencies, differentiators, relevant past performance and company data. It is useful when a full company profile is too long, or as a companion document for meetings and supplier registrations.

Support tenders with clear presentation

Tender requirements are set by the issuing organisation, and the content and compliance of a submission remain the bidder's responsibility. Tender document design and presentation support can, however, help make your submission organised, consistent and easier to evaluate, with clear section dividers, branded templates and well-formatted supporting information.

Proposals and presentations

When an opportunity reaches the proposal or meeting stage, business proposal design and corporate presentation design should follow the same brand standard as your profile. Consistency across every document reinforces that your business is organised and dependable. Our guide to presentation design systems explains how to approach this.

Keep your documents up to date

Review your company profile at least once a year, and whenever your services, team, compliance status or contact details change. Outdated information is one of the most common reasons a strong business makes a weak first impression.
